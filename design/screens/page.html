Editorial Standards

How HauntedReal reports, what we verify, and where the line sits between our journalism and the accounts our readers send us.

HauntedReal covers the paranormal as a reporting beat. That means we go to places, request records, interview the people who work there, and publish what we find — including the occasions when what we find is nothing at all.

What we publish

An investigation runs when there is something to investigate: a documented claim, an accessible site, and a method that could in principle produce a negative result. Stories that cannot fail are not investigations, they are advertisements for a legend.

We state our access conditions in every piece. If a property owner set terms, those terms appear in the story. If we were accompanied, we say by whom.

How we verify a location

Every location we name is checked against public records — deeds, historical registers, newspaper archives, and where relevant, court filings. A great many well-known American hauntings rest on dates and names that do not survive that check, and we say so plainly.

What verification does not cover

Confirming that a house existed, that a death occurred, and that a witness worked there is not the same as confirming a haunting. We are explicit about this distinction because it is the one most often blurred.

Community experiences

Reader accounts are published in a separate section, on separate templates, with a permanent label. They are edited only for length and clarity. We do not add detail, we do not dramatize, and we do not present them as reporting.

Corrections

Corrections are appended to the bottom of the affected story with the date and the nature of the change. We do not silently edit published claims.
